Diamanti vs. FusionX

Two identities, one company. This page is the first thing to check before starting any new piece of collateral: which brand does it wear, how does that brand actually look, and what happens when both are legitimately in the room. This is a production tool, not a positioning essay — it documents how Diamanti leadership's repositioning call has been applied so far, so the same judgment gets applied consistently going forward.

The short version

  • Diamanti = the company. Used for internal/legal/corporate-of-record material and anything where the reader needs to trust the operator, not just the platform. No visual refresh — stays as-is.
  • FusionX = the platform. Used for nearly everything else — internal or external, strategy or sales — anything communicating the product itself.
  • Default rule (corrected against actual shipped history): FusionX is the primary visual identity for nearly all collateral, including internal strategy documents. Every brief shipped to date, including the internal Leadership Solution Brief, uses the full FusionX visual system — Diamanti has not yet led a document's visual identity on anything shipped. Diamanti's confirmed role is narrower: cover credit ("Powered by Diamanti"), confidentiality-notice ownership on internal docs, and About-the-Company content — always subordinate to a FusionX-primary layout, never a competing visual system.

How each one looks

Diamanti — Corporate

  • Diamanti wordmark, standard lockup, no green "X" treatment
  • Corporate palette: Diamanti green #93D500, blue #2492C3 / #0074A8, navy #1B2E7A (logo only), tangerine #F15A24, dark gray #4D4D4D, black #040708
  • No full-document use confirmed yet — has not appeared as the primary visual system on any brief, deck, or internal document shipped to date
  • No topo/waveform/light-burst hero treatment — that's FusionX's language
  • Actual confirmed role: cover credit line, confidentiality-notice ownership, About-the-Company content

FusionX — Product

  • FusionX wordmark — one-word lockup for logo/visual use (two-word "Fusion X" rule in running copy is a separate, currently unconfirmed question — out of scope here pending confirmation from Chris/Prasoon)
  • Product palette: FusionX Lime #6EE600 (primary accent), Lime Electric #6DFF33 (hero highlights), Interactive Blue #008CFF, production dark background #0E180D
  • Full brief-series cover language: full-bleed atmospheric hero (topo contour / waveform / radiating light-burst, motif varies, chrome consistent), two-tone title (white + Lime), small-caps eyebrow, Lime-caps presenter block
  • Interior pages: flat-card Swiss-editorial system, generous whitespace, icon system per the FusionX icon library
  • "Powered by Diamanti" = standard subordinate credit when Diamanti needs to be present but secondary

Full token values, type ramp, icon rules, and imagery direction live on their own pages — see Color, Type, Icons, and Imagery. This page is about which identity applies, not the full spec of either.

Decision path for anything new

Is this a legal, HR, or pure-corporate document — not a strategy, sales, or product piece?

Contracts, offer letters, invoices, compliance filings, anything where the counterparty is Diamanti the legal entity → Diamanti corporate identity. Everything else confirmed to date has used FusionX, including internal leadership material. No confirmed Diamanti-branded example exists yet outside pure back-office documents.

Does the reader need reassurance about the operator, not just the product?

If yes, add Diamanti as a subordinate credit ("Powered by Diamanti," an About-the-Company section, or for internal docs a confidentiality notice naming Diamanti as document owner). This sits on top of the FusionX visual system, doesn't replace it.

If a new format doesn't fit either exception, default to FusionX and treat that as the working assumption — the burden of proof is on finding a reason to use Diamanti, not the other way around.

Where Diamanti actually appears

Cover credit

"Powered by Diamanti" or small company-of-record line under the FusionX title. Confirmed on Leadership Brief cover and the virtual-background project.

Confidentiality / ownership notice

For internal docs, a Diamanti-owned confidentiality notice sits alongside FusionX content — a legal/ops function, not a design one. Confirmed precedent: Leadership Brief.

About-the-Company section

A dedicated section inside a FusionX-primary brief can carry Diamanti corporate proof points (founded 2012, 14+ years) without switching the surrounding visual system.

What doesn't happen: two co-equal hero logos, two competing color systems on the same page, or a document defaulting to full Diamanti visual identity because the audience is internal. Internal audience is not, on its own, a reason to leave FusionX.
